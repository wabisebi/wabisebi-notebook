Same meaning: "but". けど is fine in polite speech too.

行いきたいけど、時間じかんがない。Everyday: with friends, and in polite talk too (ですけど).
行いきたいけれど、時間じかんがない。A bit more careful, also common in writing.
行いきたいですけれども、時間じかんがありません。Polite and careful: to an audience, at work.
行いきたいですが、時間じかんがありません。Formal: work, emails, news, writing.

Same meaning: "I want to go, but I don't have time." Only how formal it sounds changes.

Polite

YouTube · video

色々いろいろ選択せんたく肢しがあるんですけれども、とりあえず、まあ、魚釣さかなつりから始はじめてみましょうか。

"There are lots of options, but for now, let's start with fishing."

けど means but. It goes right after a whole sentence part, and the second half turns away from the first. けれども is the full, careful version, in the same spot.

On this card, a YouTuber talks politely to an audience: あるんです + けれども = "there are lots of options, but...".

けど is the everyday short form, fine with friends and even in polite speech (ですけど).

Casual

Teasing Master Takagi-san · Season 2, Episode 11

ウワッ…そうだけど、なんで？

"Whoa... yeah, but why?"

On this card, he admits it's true, then asks back: そうだ + けど, "that's right, but how did you know?"

Noun or な-adjective before it? Add だ first.

WordAddEnding
Noun学生がくせいだけどI'm a student, but…
な-adjective静しずかだけどIt's quiet, but…
Verb行いく–けどI'm going, but…
い-adjective高たかい–けどIt's expensive, but…

Quick Examples

A friend asks about the new ramen place.

高たかいけど、おいしいよ。

"It's pricey, but it's good."

い-adjective + けど. Nothing to add in between.

Casual

Your friend invites you out tonight.

行いきたいけど、明日あした早はやいんだ。

"I want to go, but I have an early day tomorrow."

Casual

Talking about your Japanese.

日本にほん語ごは好すきだけど、文法ぶんぽうは難むずかしい。

"I like Japanese, but grammar is hard."

After a な-adjective or a noun, add だ first: 好きだけど, 学生だけど. Without だ it sounds cut off.

Casual

At a hotel desk, asking for help.

すみません、Wi-Fiが使つかえないんですけど…

"Excuse me, the Wi-Fi isn't working... [can you help?]"

Stopping at けど makes it soft. You say the problem and let the other person offer help.

Polite

With and without

Without

この部屋へやは狭せまい。安やすい。

"This room is small. It's cheap." Two facts, no link.

With けど

この部屋へやは狭せまいけど、安やすい。

"This room is small, but it's cheap." Now the second part pushes back against the first.

Same job as が (But). が sounds a bit more formal; けど works almost everywhere.

Also the same "but" as でも, just in a different spot. けど glues onto the end of the first part, so it stays one sentence (狭いけど、安い). でも starts a fresh sentence (狭い。でも、安い).

Examples from Media

Polite

Brush Up Life · Episode 5

あっ、これペラ刷すったんですけど、いりますか？

"Oh, I printed a flyer. Want one?"

Here けど isn't really "but": it sets up what comes next. Very common before a question or a request.

Polite

Oishinbo · Episode 29

せっかくですけど、その取材しゅざいはお断ことわりします。

"Kind of you, but I'll have to turn down the interview."

せっかくですけど = "it's kind of you, but...", the polite way to say no.

Polite

I'm Taking the Day Off · Episode 4

分わかってますけど。

"I know that."

ますけど: polite, with a "so what?" feel.

Casual

Castle in the Sky

うん、伝説でんせつっていわれてたけど。

"Yeah, people said it was just a legend, though."

It ends on けど and stops: "but..." with the rest left unsaid.

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 3

新あたらしいパンダグッズ、買かっちゃったんだけど

"I bought some new panda goods, so... [want to see?]"

Here けど isn't really "but". It just opens a topic and waits for a reaction. Very common at the end of a casual sentence.

Casual

Dungeon Meshi · Episode 23

"Too bad, but..."

な-word + だ + けど.

Casual

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 29

"It's good [but...]."

The sentence stops at けど, the "but" is left unsaid.
