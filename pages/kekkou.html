N5 Grammar

けっこう (結構)

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Season 1, Episode 6

えっ、ストロング？結構けっこう、苦にがいよ。

"Huh, the strong one? It's pretty bitter, you know."

けっこう means quite, pretty: more than you'd expect, but softer than とても. It goes right before the describing word: 結構 + 苦い = "pretty bitter".

On this card, someone picks the strong one and gets a warning: it's more bitter than they might think.

結構です has a second job: a polite "No, thank you" when someone offers you something.

Polite

Quartet · Episode 2

僕ぼくは結構けっこうです。

"None for me, thanks."

結構です is also a polite "no thanks". On this card, 僕は結構です = "none for me": the others can have some.

Quick Examples

First time trying natto. Your host asks how it is.

納豆なっとう、けっこうおいしいです。

"Natto is actually pretty good."

けっこう often carries "more than I thought". You expected worse.

Polite

At the convenience store checkout.

A: 袋ふくろはいりますか。
B: いえ、結構けっこうです。

A: "Do you need a bag?" B: "No, I'm fine."

結構です here means "no thanks". The いえ in front makes it clear.

Polite

Halfway up a hill on a long walk with a friend.

けっこう歩あるいたね。

"We've walked quite a lot."

With a verb, けっこう means "quite a lot".

Casual

Looking at a flat with a friend.

この部屋へや、けっこう広ひろいね。

"This room is pretty big."

Casual

With and without

Without

この映画えいがは面白おもしろかった。

"This movie was interesting." A plain opinion.

With けっこう

この映画えいがはけっこう面白おもしろかった。

"This movie was pretty interesting." Better than you expected.

Examples from Media

Polite

YouTube · video

結構けっこう、エンジニアにも人気にんきのブランドっすよね。

"It's a brand that's pretty popular with engineers too, right?"

結構 + 人気 ("popular"). っす is a relaxed way to say です.
