N5 Grammar

へ

Polite

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 34

お好すきな席せきへどうぞ。

"Please go to any seat you like."

へ (said "e") marks the direction something moves in: toward. It goes right after a place or a direction word: 席 + へ.

On this card, you're pointed toward any seat you like. It answers "Go which way?": to a seat. どうぞ ("please") keeps it polite.

Same in polite and casual speech. With 行く, 来る, 帰る you can use へ or に: へ leans on the direction, に on the spot you land.

Casual

How do you like Wednesday? · Episode 1

深夜しんやバス、オレンジライナー、東京とうきょうから西にしへ約やく900キロ。

"The overnight bus, the Orange Liner, [heading] about 900 km west from Tokyo."

On this card, there's no verb at all, and へ still tells you the bus is heading somewhere: 西へ, west.

Quick Examples

Leaving the office, a coworker asks where you're off to.

駅えきへ行いきます。

"I'm heading to the station."

駅に行きます means the same thing. With 行く, either one is fine.

Polite

Watching birds fly over in autumn.

鳥とりが南みなみへ飛とんでいく。

"The birds are flying south."

No destination, just a direction. That's へ's home turf.

Casual

It's late, and the party is winding down.

そろそろ家いえへ帰かえろう。

"Let's head home soon."

家へ帰る: going back toward home. 家に帰ろう works too.

Casual

You thought you were meeting at the station, but your friend texts "I'll come to your place tonight."

ここへ？

"[You're coming] here?"

The verb (来るの) is dropped because it's obvious. へ alone says "coming this way". Just ここ？ could mean anything.

Casual

With and without

に: the spot you land

京都きょうとに着ついた。

"I arrived in Kyoto." The point is that you're there now.

へ: the way you're going

京都きょうとへ向むかった。

"I headed for Kyoto." The point is the direction. You might still be on the way.
