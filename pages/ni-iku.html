N5 Grammar

～に行く・～に来る

～に行く means go to do something. It answers "going to do what?".

Take the verb's ます-form, drop ます, add に行く: します → しに行く, 食べます → 食べに行く. Activity nouns work too: 買い物に行く, スキーに行く.

With 来る it's "come to do something": 遊びに来る. Polite: に行きます, に来ます. Casual: に行く, に来る.

Quick Examples

You're heading out. You tell your roommate where you're going.

コンビニに飲のみ物ものを買かいに行いくね。

"I'm going to the convenience store to get drinks."

Two に: コンビニに = where, 買いに = to do what.

Casual

Monday at work. A coworker asks about your weekend.

海うみに泳およぎに行いきました。

"I went to the beach for a swim."

泳ぎます → 泳ぎ + に行きました.

Polite

Your friend shows up at your door without warning.

え、何なにしに来きたの？

"Huh, what are you here for?"

Literally "came to do what?". 何しに is a very common question.

Casual

At a party, someone asks why you came to Japan.

日本にほん語ごを勉強べんきょうしに来きました。

"I came to study Japanese."

You're in Japan now, so it's 来る, not 行く.

Polite

With and without

Without

図書としょ館かんに行いきます。

"I'm going to the library." Only where.

With ～に行く

図書としょ館かんに本ほんを返かえしに行いきます。

"I'm going to the library to return a book." Where, and what for.
