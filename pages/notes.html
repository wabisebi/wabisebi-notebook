Polite vs careful: です and では

Japanese has two separate switches.

1. Polite or not: is there a です at the end? This is about who you're talking to. Add です and you're being polite to the listener. Leave it out and you're not.

2. Relaxed or careful: じゃ or では? This is about how carefully you word it. じゃ is the squashed, spoken version. では is the full version. Like English "wasn't" vs "was not": same meaning, and "was not" isn't more polite, just more careful.

The two switches mix freely:

Relaxed (じゃ)Careful (では)
No ですじゃなかったではなかった
With ですじゃなかったですではありませんでした

じゃなかった: talking to a friend.
ではなかった: books, articles, a big dramatic line. Nobody to be polite to, but carefully worded.
じゃなかったです: polite, but friendly and relaxed.
ではありませんでした: polite and formal, like news or speeches.

Why ではなかった is under "Casual" on the じゃなかった page. On the site, "Casual" just means no です. It doesn't mean relaxed. ではなかった has no です, so it goes there, even though it sounds careful.

Once です is at the end, it's always polite. Some polite forms are just softer than others.
