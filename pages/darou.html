N5 Grammar

だろう

He Who Can't Marry · Episode 1

俺の受け売りだろう？

"That's [just] what I told you, right?"

だろう does the same job as でしょう: "probably", or with a rising voice "right?". It goes at the very end, after a plain verb, an い-adjective, or a noun (no だ before it).

Here he's pretty sure where the other guy got that idea, so it's "right?". Said out loud to someone, だろう and the short だろ sound masculine and a bit blunt. Most women, and plenty of men, say でしょ instead.

Two places where だろう is neutral and everyone uses it: "I wonder..." (なんだろう, said to yourself), and writing or inner thoughts.

Examples

You look at the dark clouds and say it to yourself.

明日も雨だろうな。

"It'll probably rain tomorrow too."

Talking to yourself, so it's fine for anyone. な makes it a thought.

Two guys after football practice.

疲れただろ？

"You're tired, right?"

Masculine. A woman would say 疲れたでしょ？

You find a strange box on your doorstep.

これ、なんだろう？

"I wonder what this is."

Question word + だろう: "I wonder". Used by everyone.

Your friend is late and not answering.

どこにいるんだろう。

"I wonder where they are."

With and without

だろ？

疲れただろ？

"You're tired, right?" Masculine, a bit rough.

でしょ？

疲れたでしょ？

Same meaning. Neutral, anyone can say it.

Without

これ、なに？

"What's this?" Asking someone.

With だろう

これ、なんだろう？

"I wonder what this is." Asking yourself, nobody needs to answer.

"I wonder how I can win her heart."

Question word (どうやったら) + んだろう: "I wonder...", said to himself.
