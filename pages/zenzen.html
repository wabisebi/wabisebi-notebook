N5 Grammar

全然～ない

YouTube · video

とにかく最初は漫画の作り方も全然知らなくって

"Anyway, at first I didn't know anything about how to make manga either."

全然 (ぜんぜん) + a negative means not at all. It goes before the part it's about, and the sentence ends negative: 知らない, not 知る.

The sentence answers "Did you know how manga is made?": no, nothing. It's the strong version of あまり: あまり知らない is "don't know much", 全然知らない is "don't know at all".

全然 never changes. Polite or casual shows at the end: 全然知りません / 全然知らない. In everyday talk you'll also hear 全然大丈夫 ("totally fine"): no negative, but it still answers a worry with "not at all".

Examples

A friend asks if you slept well before the exam.

全然寝てない。

"I didn't sleep at all."

Casual

A coworker asks if you speak Korean.

全然話せません。

"I can't speak it at all."

Polite

Your friend shows you a math problem.

全然分からない。

"I have no idea."

Casual

A coworker apologizes for being five minutes late.

全然大丈夫ですよ。

"It's totally fine."

The everyday 全然: no negative, but it answers "Is it a problem?" with "not at all".

Polite

With and without

With あまり

あまり分からない。

"I don't really understand." A little, not much.

With 全然

全然分からない。

"I don't understand at all." Nothing.

Polite

全然分かりません。

Same meaning, polite.

Not this

全然分かる。

全然 needs a negative (or 大丈夫). Without it the sentence breaks.

もう顔もほぼすっぴんなんであの全然

"My face is basically bare anyway, so, um, not at all."

The rest is left unsaid. 全然 alone means "not at all, no problem".
