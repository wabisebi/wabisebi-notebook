N5 Grammar

全然～ない

Polite

When Marnie Was There

全然ぜんぜんかまいません。

"I don't mind at all."

全然 (ぜんぜん) + a negative means not at all. It goes before the part it's about, and the sentence ends negative.

On this card, he says sorry the car is so cramped, and she answers: 全然 + かまいません ("don't mind"). It's the strong version of あまり: あまり知らない is "don't know much", 全然知らない is "don't know at all".

全然 never changes. Polite or casual shows at the end. In everyday talk you'll also hear 全然大丈夫 ("totally fine"): no negative, but it still answers a worry with "not at all".

Casual

YouTube · video

とにかく最初さいしょは漫画まんがの作つくり方かたも全然ぜんぜん知しらなくって

"Anyway, at first I didn't know anything about how to make manga either."

On this card, 全然知らなくって = "didn't know anything at all" about how manga is made.

Quick Examples

A friend asks if you slept well before the exam.

全然ぜんぜん寝ねてない。

"I didn't sleep at all."

Casual

A coworker asks if you speak Korean.

全然ぜんぜん話はなせません。

"I can't speak it at all."

Polite

Your friend shows you a math problem.

全然ぜんぜん分わからない。

"I have no idea."

Casual

A coworker apologizes for being five minutes late.

全然ぜんぜん大丈夫だいじょうぶですよ。

"It's totally fine."

The everyday 全然: no negative, but it answers "Is it a problem?" with "not at all".

Polite

With and without

With あまり

あまり分わからない。

"I don't really understand." A little, not much.

With 全然

全然ぜんぜん分わからない。

"I don't understand at all." Nothing.

Polite

全然ぜんぜん分わかりません。

Same meaning, polite.

Not this

全然ぜんぜん分わかる。

全然 needs a negative (or 大丈夫). Without it the sentence breaks.
