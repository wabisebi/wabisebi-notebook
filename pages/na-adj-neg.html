N5 Grammar

な-Adjective (Negative)

Shirokuma Cafe · Season 1, Episode 4

ちっとも便利じゃないわねスマホ

"Smartphones aren't handy at all, are they?"

To say a な-adjective is not, add じゃない right after it, no な: 便利 (handy) → 便利じゃない. It works exactly like a noun: 学生じゃない.

On this card, the speaker is fed up with her phone. The sentence answers "Are smartphones handy?": not one bit. ちっとも is "not at all", and わね is a soft "aren't they".

Polite: 便利じゃないです, or a bit stiffer 便利じゃありません. Watch out: 綺麗 and 嫌い end in い but are な-adjectives, so 綺麗じゃない, never 綺麗くない.

Examples

A friend picks up the new game you've been playing and asks if it's easy.

簡単じゃないよ。

"It's not easy."

簡単 (easy) + じゃない.

Casual

A coworker asks if you can help out tonight.

すみません、今日は暇じゃないです。

"Sorry, I'm not free today."

Polite

A friend offers you natto.

ごめん、納豆は好きじゃない。

"Sorry, I don't like natto."

好き is a な-adjective too, so 好きじゃない.

Casual

The hotel staff asks how your room is.

あまり静かじゃありません。

"It's not very quiet."

じゃありません is the stiffer polite one. Same meaning as じゃないです.

Polite

With and without

Is

静かだ。

"It's quiet."

Isn't

静かじゃない。

"It's not quiet." Just add じゃない.

Polite

静かじゃないです。

Same thing, polite. Also fine: 静かじゃありません.

Not this

静かくない。

くない is for い-adjectives (高い → 高くない). な-adjectives use じゃない.

"His plan isn't realistic at all."

現実的 (realistic) is a な-adjective. まったく is "not at all", like 全然.

"I don't like sweet things."

好き is a な-adjective: 好きじゃない. んです makes it polite and explains.
