N5 Grammar

ぐらい・くらい

Polite

YouTube · video

フレーバーの紅茶で、100種類ぐらいあるんですよ！

"There are about 100 kinds of flavored tea!"

ぐらい means about, roughly. It goes right after an amount: 100種類 + ぐらい = "about 100 kinds".

She didn't count them. ぐらい tells you the number is a rough guess. It answers "How many?": around 100.

くらい and ぐらい are the same word. Say whichever comes out, both are fine in polite and casual speech.

Casual

YouTube · video

最初の1時間は無料なんだけど、それ以降は1時間100円くらい。

"The first hour is free, but after that it's about 100 yen an hour."

Same thing with くらい: 100円くらい = "about 100 yen". Casual, so no です at the end.

Examples

Someone asks how long you've studied Japanese.

3年ぐらいです。

"About three years."

Without ぐらい it's exactly three years. With it, "give or take".

Polite

Asking the hotel how far the station is.

駅まで歩いてどのくらいかかりますか？

"How long does it take to walk to the station?"

どの + くらい = "about how much?": the question version. どれくらい means the same. Answer: 10分ぐらいです.

Polite

Your friend is cooking rice and asks you.

ご飯、どれくらい食べる？

"How much rice do you want?"

You can answer with your hands: このくらい. "About this much."

Casual

A friend asks how much your new bike cost.

5万円くらいかな。

"About 50,000 yen, I think."

くらい + かな: you're not quite sure of the price.

Casual

With and without

Without

駅まで10分かかります。

"It takes 10 minutes to the station." Sounds exact.

With ぐらい

駅まで10分ぐらいかかります。

"It takes about 10 minutes to the station." A rough guess.

Sentence mining

Polite

YouTube · video

モントリオールから、車で１時間ぐらいのところです。

"It's a place about an hour by car from Montreal."

1時間ぐらいのところ: "a place about an hour away". ぐらい + の before a noun.

Casual

YouTube · video

ちょっと雪も降りました。ちょっとね。このぐらいかな。

"It snowed a little too. Just a bit. About this much, I guess."

このぐらい: "about this much", while showing it. Polite start, casual かな at the end.
