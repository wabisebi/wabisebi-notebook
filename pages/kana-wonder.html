N4 Grammar

かな

Polite～でしょうか a different word, same "I wonder"
～かなと思います かな tucked inside, "I'm thinking maybe..."
Casual～かな

かな on its own is always casual. To sound polite, swap it for でしょうか, or keep it and end with と思います.

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 39

ゆずこしょう どこだったかな

"Where was the yuzu pepper again?"

かな means "I wonder". You're thinking out loud, half to yourself, and nobody has to answer.

It goes at the end of a casual sentence: a verb, an adjective or a noun (どこだった + かな, セイウチかな). On this card, he's looking around the kitchen and asking himself where the yuzu pepper is.

It's casual. In polite speech you'd say でしょうか instead. With ～てくれるかな or ～てもいいかな it becomes a soft request: "could you...?"

Examples

Looking out the window in the morning.

今日きょうは雨あめかな。

"Will it rain today, I wonder."

After a noun, no だ.

Casual

At the menu, deciding.

カレーにしようかな。

"Maybe I'll go with curry."

～ようかな: deciding out loud, "maybe I'll...".

Casual

Asking a friend for a small favor.

ちょっと手伝てつだってくれるかな？

"Could you help me a bit?"

A soft way to ask.

Casual

With and without

With か

彼かれは来くるか？

Is he coming? A real question, you want an answer.

With かな

彼かれは来くるかな。

I wonder if he's coming. Just thinking out loud.
