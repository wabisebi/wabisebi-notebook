のは・のが

Noun or な-adjective before it? Add な first.

昼間ひるまに山道さんどうを歩あるくのは楽たのしいです

"Walking along the mountain path during the day is fun."

の turns an action into a thing. 歩く "walk" → 歩くの "walking" (as in "walking is fun", not "I'm walking").

On this card, the whole shaded chunk 昼間に山道を歩く becomes one thing, and は makes it the topic: "walking the mountain path in the daytime" is fun.

のが works the same way: 歩くのが好き, "I like walking".

今日きょう行いくのは、逗子ずしという街まちだ。

"Where I'm going today is a town called Zushi."

のは turns a verb into "the place or thing that...", and the answer comes last. On this card, 今日行くのは = "the place I'm going today", and casual だ ends it.

Examples

A friend asks what you do on weekends. You love swimming.

泳およぐのが好すき。

"I like swimming."

の works like English "-ing": swim becomes swimming.

の = the act

Your alarm goes off at 5am, it's dark and cold.

朝あさ早はやく起おきるのはつらい。

"Getting up early is rough."

The whole chunk 朝早く起きる becomes one thing, then は: as for that, it's rough.

の = the act

At a party, someone asks who made the cake.

ケーキを作つくったのは母ははだ。

"The one who made the cake is my mom."

Everyone knows someone made it. The answer comes last, like a reveal.

の = the one

Back from a trip to Japan, a friend asks about the food.

一番いちばんおいしかったのはラーメンだった。

"The thing that was tastiest was the ramen."

の = the thing

With and without

Not this

泳およぐが好すき。

Broken. Like "I like swim". が needs a thing, and 泳ぐ is an action.

With の

泳およぐのが好すき。

"I like swimming." の turns the action into a thing.

Without

母ははがケーキを作つくった。

"Mom made the cake." Just the plain fact.

With の

ケーキを作つくったのは母ははだ。

"The one who made the cake? Mom." Same fact, but the answer is saved for the end.

When to add な

Noun or な-adjective right before の? Put な in between. Verbs and い-adjectives get nothing.

Verb / い-adjective

泳およぐのが好すき。　寒さむいのが苦手にがて。

Nothing added.

Noun / な-adjective

静しずかなのがいい。

"Quiet is good." Add な.

飛行ひこう機きが着陸ちゃくりくするのが見みえますね。

"You can see the plane landing."

見える (can see) needs a thing in front of が, like 山が見える. 着陸する (to land) is a verb, so it gets の first: "the landing".
