N5 Grammar

ね

Polite

My Neighbor Totoro

こんにちは！ご精せいが出でますね。

"Hello! Working hard, I see."

ね at the end of a sentence means "you see it too, right?". It goes on the very end, after です, ます or a plain verb or adjective.

On this card, someone greets a neighbor busy at work. It isn't news, the neighbor knows they're working hard. ね just shares the moment.

Polite: ですね / ますね. Casual: drop です, but after a noun or な-adjective add だ: 大変だね.

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Season 1, Episode 5

楽たのしそうだね

"That sounds fun, huh."

そう ("looks like") works like a な-adjective, so it gets だ before ね.

Casual, after a noun or な-adjective? Add だ first.

WordAddEnding
Noun学生がくせいだねYou're a student, huh.
な-adjective静しずかだねIt's quiet, huh.
Verb行いく–ねYou're going, huh.
い-adjective高たかい–ねIt's expensive, huh.

Quick Examples

You and a friend step outside. It's freezing.

寒さむいね

"It's cold, huh."

Casual

You're both eating the curry your friend made. It's really good.

これ、おいしいね

"This is good, isn't it."

Casual

Small talk with a neighbor while it pours outside.

よく降おりますね

"It sure is raining a lot."

Polite

A coworker shows you a photo of their new apartment.

広ひろい部屋へやですね

"What a big room."

Polite

With and without

Without

寒さむい

"It's cold." Just a statement, to yourself or anyone.

With ね

寒さむいね

"It's cold, huh." You both feel it, and you're sharing that.

With よ

寒さむいよ

"It's cold, you know." They don't know yet, so you tell them (maybe they're about to go out without a coat).

With ね

広ひろい部屋へやですね

"What a big room." You're both looking at it.

Examples from Media

Polite

"That's the worst."

ね: she expects the listener to agree.

Polite

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 15

"That's a dogū figurine, huh."

"I'm going to bed, okay?"
