N5 Grammar

を

Polite

Source unknown

炭酸たんさん水すいを買かいます

"[I'll] buy sparkling water."

を marks the object: the thing the action is done to. It goes right after that noun, before the verb: 炭酸水 + を + 買います.

On this card, 買います is "buy", and を tells you what gets bought. It answers "Buy what?": sparkling water. There's no "I": it's clear who's buying, so Japanese leaves it out (the [brackets]).

Written を, said "o". In casual speech it's often dropped: 炭酸水買う.

Casual

YouTube · video

店員てんいんさんが注文ちゅうもんを聞きいているよ。

"The clerk is taking the order."

を marks the thing the action is done to. On this card, the clerk is asking for the order, so 注文 gets を.

With and without

With を

コーヒーを飲のみますか？

Polite, complete. Shops, clients.

Dropped

コーヒー飲のむ？

With friends を often just disappears. Totally fine.

"Finally, I sprinkled on bonito flakes."

What did I sprinkle? The bonito flakes, so を.

YouTube · video

魚さかなの鱗うろこを取とっています。

"I'm removing the fish scales."

The thing being removed: 魚の鱗 (the fish's scales).

Polite

YouTube · video

玉たまねぎを切きると涙なみだが出でますよ。

"When you cut onions, you tear up."

玉ねぎを切る: cut the onion. The tears get が: nobody is doing something to them, they just come out.

Polite

"Please attach a photo of your face."

Same shape as コーヒーをください: thing + を, then (て)ください.
