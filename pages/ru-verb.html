る-Verb (Dictionary)

Polite～ます drop る, add ます
Casual～る dictionary form

Same meaning. Only the politeness changes.

Japanese has three types of verbs. This page is about the second one, the る-verbs.

Verb typeDictionaryPolite
1う-verbmost verbs書くalso 飲む, 話す, 買う...書きますu sound → i sound, add ます
2る-verbthis page食べるalso 見る, 起きる, 寝る...食べますdrop る, add ます
3Irregularjust these two verbsする
来る		します
来ますjust learn these
How to tell which type a verb is
  1. Step 1

    Is it する or 来る?

    Yes: Irregular. No: go to step 2.

  2. Step 2

    Does it end in る?

    No: う-verb (書く, 飲む). Yes: go to step 3.

  3. Step 3

    Is the sound just before る an i or an e?

    No: う-verb (分かる wa-ka-ru, 作る tsu-ku-ru).

    Yes: usually a る-verb (見る mi-ru, 食べる ta-be-ru).

A few break step 3. These look like る-verbs but are う-verbs: 帰る, 入る, 走る, 知る.

Polite

YouTube · video

これは、すごく共感きょうかんできます。

"I can really relate to this."

できる is the dictionary form of a る-verb: the verb exactly as the dictionary lists it, ending in る. Said like this, it's casual.

Polite: drop the る and add ます: できます. Nothing else moves, and that's what makes it a る-verb.

On this card, 共感できる ("can relate") becomes 共感できます.

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 20

モダン焼やき、できる？

"Can you make modan-yaki?"

The dictionary form alone is casual. On this card, it answers "Is that possible?": モダン焼、できる？ As a casual question, you just raise your voice at the end.

A verb alone can be a sentence

Japanese doesn't need a subject, so one verb can be the whole sentence. The context tells you who's doing it.

Kekkon Dekinai Otoko · Episode 6

やめる

"[I] quit."

Casual

Demon Slayer - Kimetsu no Yaiba · Episode 6

Casual

Good Morning Call · Episode 13

Quick Examples

A friend asks about your Sunday mornings.

日曜にちよう日ひは、昼ひるまで寝ねるよ。

"On Sundays I sleep till noon."

寝る (to sleep). Polite: 寝ます.

Casual

Asking a coworker about lunch.

いつもここで昼ひるご飯はんを食たべますか。

"Do you always eat lunch here?"

食べる → 食べます.

Polite

Your friend asks what you're doing tonight.

今夜こんやは家いえで映画えいがを見みる。

"I'm watching a movie at home tonight."

The dictionary form also works for the future.

Casual

Telling your teacher about your mornings.

毎朝まいあさ六ろく時じに起おきます。

"I get up at six every morning."

起きる → 起きます.

Polite
