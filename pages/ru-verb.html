N5 Grammar

る-Verb (Dictionary)

Polite食べます
Casual食べる

Same meaning: "to eat". Only the politeness changes.

る-verbs are the second big group of Japanese verbs. Their dictionary form always ends in る, and the sound right before it is an i or an e sound: 見る (miru), 食べる (taberu).

In Japanese they're called 一段 (ichidan), "one step": the front of the verb never changes. To use one, drop the る and add the ending: 食べ + ます, 食べ + ない, 食べ + た.

Watch out: a few verbs look like this but are う-verbs: 帰る, 入る, 走る, 知る. Those you just have to learn.

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 20

モダン焼、できる？

"Can you make modan-yaki?"

できる is the dictionary form of a る-verb: the verb exactly as the dictionary lists it, ending in る. Said like this, it's casual.

On this card, it answers "Is that possible?". As a casual question, you just raise your voice at the end, no か needed.

Polite: drop the る and add ます: できます. Nothing else moves, and that's what makes it a る-verb.

Polite

YouTube · video

これは、すごく共感できます。

"I can really relate to this."

Same verb, polite: できる → できます. 共感できる = "can relate".

Examples

A friend asks about your Sunday mornings.

日曜日は、昼まで寝るよ。

"On Sundays I sleep till noon."

寝る (to sleep). Polite: 寝ます.

Casual

Asking a coworker about lunch.

いつもここで昼ご飯を食べますか。

"Do you always eat lunch here?"

食べる → 食べます.

Polite

Your friend asks what you're doing tonight.

今夜は家で映画を見る。

"I'm watching a movie at home tonight."

The dictionary form also works for the future.

Casual

Telling your teacher about your mornings.

毎朝六時に起きます。

"I get up at six every morning."

起きる → 起きます.

Polite

With and without

Without

パンを食べます。

"I eat bread." Polite, to a teacher or someone you don't know well.

With 食べる

パンを食べる。

"I eat bread." Same meaning, casual, to a friend.
