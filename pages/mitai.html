N4 Grammar

みたい

Polite～みたいです
Casual～みたいだ・～みたい

Same meaning: "seems like". Only the politeness changes.

Nothing to add, not even の or な after a noun.

WordAddEnding
Noun学生がくせい–みたいSeems like a student
な-adjective静しずか–みたいSeems quiet
Verb行いく–みたいSeems he's going
い-adjective高たかい–みたいSeems expensive
Polite

Aggretsuko · Season 2, Episode 10

只野ただのくんは結婚けっこんするつもりはないみたいです。

"Tadano doesn't seem to plan on getting married."

みたい means "seems like, looks like": you're saying what it looks like from what you see or hear, without being sure.

It goes straight after a verb, an adjective or a noun (ない + みたい, 雨みたい). On this card, she hasn't asked him straight out; she's guessing from how he talks.

After a noun it can also mean "like" (a comparison): 夢みたい, "like a dream".

Casual

Shirokuma Cafe · Episode 19

ああ まぁ エゾリスたちとは 打うち解とけたみたいだな

"Yeah, well, looks like he's warmed up to the squirrels."

On this card, he guesses from what he sees: they seem to get along now. Casual drops です; みたいだな adds a thinking-out-loud feel.

Quick Examples

You look out the window, the street is wet.

雨あめが降ふったみたい。

"Looks like it rained."

You didn't see it rain, the street tells you.

Casual

A colleague's desk has been empty all day.

田中たなかさんは休やすみみたいです。

"Looks like Tanaka is off today."

After a noun, no の or な: 休みみたい.

Polite

You taste a homemade dish.

お店みせみたいな味あじだね！

"It tastes like a restaurant!"

みたいな + noun: "a ... like ...".

Casual

With and without

Without

田中たなかさんは休やすみです。

Tanaka is off. You know it.

With みたい

田中たなかさんは休やすみみたいです。

Tanaka seems to be off. That's how it looks, you're not sure.
