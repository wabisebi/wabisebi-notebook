English verb, Japanese noun

Your friend's concert is tomorrow and you can't wait. English says it with a verb or an adjective: "I'm looking forward to tomorrow." "I'm excited."

Japanese says it with a noun:

明日が楽しみです。

楽しみ is a thing, something like "a joy to look forward to". So the sentence is built like 明日は学生です: noun + です. Literally "tomorrow is a looking-forward-thing".

JapaneseEnglish
い-adjective明日は楽しい。Tomorrow will be fun. describes the day
Noun明日が楽しみ。I can't wait for tomorrow. your feeling of waiting

More of the same. In English these are verbs or adjectives, in Japanese just noun + です:

JapaneseEnglishLiterally
心配です。I'm worried.It's worry.
明日は仕事です。I'm working tomorrow.Tomorrow is work.
父は留守です。Dad's not home.Dad is absence.

No rule tells you 楽しみ is a noun and 楽しい an adjective. You get used to it by hearing it.

To remember: learn the whole chunk, then swap words in. 〇〇が楽しみです = "I can't wait for 〇〇": 旅行が楽しみです, 週末が楽しみです.
