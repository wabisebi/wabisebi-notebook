N4 Grammar

～そうだ (Heard)

Polite～そうです
Casual～そうだ

Same meaning: "I heard that". Only the politeness changes.

Noun or な-adjective? Add だ. Even in polite speech.

WordAddEnding
Noun学生がくせいだそうだI hear he's a student
な-adjective静しずかだそうだI hear it's quiet
Verb行いく–そうだI hear he's going
い-adjective高たかい–そうだI hear it's expensive
Polite

Quartet · Episode 1

人生じんせいには ３つの坂さかがあるそうです

"They say there are three hills in life."

This ～そうだ passes on something you heard or read: "I heard", "they say". It's not your own guess, it's someone else's information.

It goes after the plain form: verbs and い-adjectives as they are (あるそうだ), nouns and な-adjectives with だ (休みだそうだ). On this card, he quotes a saying he's heard: life has three hills.

Don't mix it up with the "looks like" そう: おいしそう is your guess from looking, おいしいそうだ is what someone told you.

Casual

1 Litre of Tears · Episode 5

回復かいふくには 少すこし時間じかんがかかるそうだ

"I'm told her recovery will take a little time."

On this card, the teacher tells the class what the doctors said about Aya. Plain そうだ sounds a bit stiff in speech: news, announcements, passing on facts.

Quick Examples

You checked the weather forecast.

明日あしたは雪ゆきが降ふるそうです。

"I hear it's going to snow tomorrow."

Polite

Sharing news with a friend.

田中たなかさん、結婚けっこんするそうだよ。

"I heard Tanaka is getting married."

Casual

A new restaurant opened near work.

あの店みせ、すごく安やすいそうですよ。

"I hear that place is really cheap."

Polite

With and without

Without

雨あめが降ふりそう。

Looks like it's going to rain. You see the clouds.

With そうだ (heard)

雨あめが降ふるそうだ。

I hear it's going to rain. The forecast said so.

Casual

Casual

Casual
