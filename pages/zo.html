Non-JLPT Grammar

ぞ

Casual

Devilman Crybaby · Episode 2

本物ほんものの銃じゅうだぞ！

"This is a real gun!"

ぞ is like よ, but much stronger: "I'm telling you!" It pushes the point at the listener, or psyches yourself up.

It goes at the very end of a casual sentence (だ + ぞ, 行く + ぞ). On this card, he wants them to take it seriously: it's a real gun.

It sounds rough and masculine, so men use it with friends, family or people below them. Never in polite speech, and the JLPT lists leave it out.

Examples

Leaving the house with your kids.

行いくぞ！

"Let's go!" / "I'm going!"

The classic one. Telling everyone it's time.

Casual

Warning a friend who's on his phone at the crossing.

危あぶないぞ！

"Watch out!"

A warning with force.

Casual

Before a big exam, to yourself.

頑張がんばるぞ！

"I'm going to do this!"

Psyching yourself up.

Casual

With and without

With よ

遅刻ちこくするよ。

You'll be late, you know. A friendly heads-up.

With ぞ

遅刻ちこくするぞ。

You'll be late! Stronger, almost an order to hurry.
