N5 Grammar

Verb + Noun

Polite～です・～ます at the end of the sentence
Casualno です or ます だ, the dictionary form, or nothing

This grammar has no polite or casual version: it looks the same either way. Only the end of the sentence sets the politeness.

Verb + Noun

A verb right before a noun describes that noun. Nothing goes in between: no の, no な.

買った本 = "the book I bought"
飲んでいる人 = "the person drinking"
住んでいるところ = "the place where I live"

Casual

YouTube · video

今住んでいるところは千葉県。

"Where I live now is Chiba."

On this card, 住んでいる (live) + ところ (place) = the place where I live. It answers "Which place?": the one I live in now.

In polite speech it's still 住んでいるところです, never ます before the noun. Only the end of the sentence shows the politeness.

Polite

YouTube · video

2025年に、大ヒットした映画があります。

"There's a movie that was a huge hit in 2025."

Past works too: 大ヒットした (was a big hit) + 映画 = a movie that was a big hit. Polite only because of あります at the end.

Examples

At a party, pointing at someone.

あそこでビールを飲んでいる人は誰？

"Who's that guy drinking beer over there?"

Everything before 人 describes the person.

Casual

Telling a coworker about a book.

昨日買った本はとても面白いです。

"The book I bought yesterday is really good."

買った (bought) + 本 = the book I bought.

Polite

Looking at a menu with a friend.

食べたいもの、ある？

"Anything you want to eat?"

たい works too: 食べたい + もの = a thing I want to eat.

Casual

Asking a station worker.

新宿に行く電車はどれですか。

"Which train goes to Shinjuku?"

行く + 電車 = the train that goes.

Polite

With and without

Without

これは本です。

"This is a book." Just some book.

With Verb + Noun

これは昨日買った本です。

"This is the book I bought yesterday." The verb tells you which book.
