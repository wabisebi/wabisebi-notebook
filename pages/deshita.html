N5 Grammar

でした・だった

Politeでした
Casualだった

Same meaning: "was". Only the politeness changes.

Polite

YouTube · Let's learn Japanese in the city!!

これは昔、山形県の県庁でした。

"This used to be the Yamagata prefectural office."

でした is the past of です: it says what something was. It goes in the same spot, right after a noun. Thing + でした = "it was (a) thing".

The guide stands in front of an old building and tells you what it used to be. 昔 ("long ago") sets the time, でした closes it in the past.

Polite: でした. Casual: だった, the past of だ. Same meaning.

Casual

New Atashin'chi · Episode 1

ほーら楽勝…だった。

"See? Piece of cake... [it] was."

Casual version: だった. Mom squeezes onto a packed train: 楽勝 ("a breeze") + だった = "it was a breeze". Her squashed face says otherwise.

Examples

A coworker asks what you did before design.

前は先生でした。

"I used to be a teacher."

Your friend asks the same thing over drinks.

前は先生だったよ。

"I used to be a teacher."

The hotel staff asks about the weather on your trip.

ずっと雨でした。

"It rained the whole time."

Literally "it was rain the whole time".

You check the old receipt. The coffee was free.

あ、タダだった。

"Oh, it was free."

With and without

だ

雨だ。

"It's raining." Now.

だった

雨だった。

"It was raining." Before. Casual.

です

雨です。

"It's raining." Now. Polite.

でした

雨でした。

"It was raining." Before. Polite. Never だったです: the past is already in でした.

In writing, じゃ becomes では.

Sentence mining

Polite

YouTube · video

じゃあ、おまけでした。

"Okay, that was the bonus."

He wraps up an extra clip. Once something is over, it becomes でした.

Polite

YouTube · video

あの、なんかちょっとあの、現代的な美術館でしたね。

"Um, it was kind of a modern art museum, huh."

でした + ね: looking back on it together.

Polite

YouTube · video

そう、でも食べる時間がバラバラだったんですよ。

"Right, but meal times were all over the place."

A polite sentence (んです at the end), but the だった in the middle stays plain. That's normal: only the end needs to be polite.
