N5 Grammar

上手・下手

Examples

Your friend sings at karaoke and nails it.

歌うたが上手じょうずだね！

"You're good at singing!"

The thing someone is good at + が + 上手. Same as 好き: が, not を.

Casual

A coworker asks if you cook at home.

いえ、料理りょうりが下手へたなんです。

"No, I'm bad at cooking."

下手 about yourself is fine. It sounds modest.

Polite

You watch a kid at the pool.

泳およぐのが上手じょうずですね。

"He's good at swimming."

With a verb, add の first: 泳ぐ + の + が. The の turns the action into a noun, because が can't come right after a verb. 泳ぐが上手 is wrong.

Polite

Your friend can't fix their bike.

もっと上手じょうずな人ひとに聞きいて。

"Ask someone who's better at it."

上手 and 下手 are な-adjectives, so before a noun they take な: 上手な人, a skilled person.

Casual

At a job interview: "What are you good at?"

料理りょうりが得意とくいです。

"Cooking is my strong point."

Don't say 上手 about yourself, it sounds like bragging. Say 得意 (my strong point) instead. The opposite is 苦手 (my weak point, "not my thing"). Both work like 上手: が before, な before a noun.

Polite

With and without

Without

歌うたが好すきだね。

"You like singing." Says nothing about how well.

With 上手

歌うたが上手じょうずだね。

"You're good at singing." Now it's about skill.

Without

毎日まいにち泳およぎます。

"I swim every day." Just what you do.

With のが下手

泳およぐのが下手へたです。

"I'm bad at swimming." Verb + の + が + 下手.
