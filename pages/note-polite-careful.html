Sounding formal isn't being polite

ではなかった sounds stiff and formal. But it isn't polite. In Japanese, those are two different things:

Polite = です at the end. Respect for the person you're talking to.
Formal-sounding = では instead of じゃ. Just the wording. (じゃ is では said quickly.)

休みじゃなかった。

"It wasn't a day off." Here it is four ways. All four mean the same thing.

Sounds relaxedSounds formal
Not politeじゃなかったではなかった
Politeじゃなかったですではなかったです

English works the same way. Think of "sir" as です:

Sounds relaxedSounds formal
Not politeIt wasn't.It was not.
PoliteIt wasn't, sir.It was not, sir.

"It was not." sounds formal, but it isn't polite. Same with ではなかった.

To remember: only です makes it polite. じゃ or では only changes how it sounds.
