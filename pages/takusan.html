N5 Grammar

たくさん

Polite～です・～ます at the end of the sentence
Casualno です or ます だ, the dictionary form, or nothing

This grammar has no polite or casual version: it looks the same either way. Only the end of the sentence sets the politeness.

Polite

YouTube · video

今日は、今からアジをたくさん釣りたいですね。

"Today I want to catch lots of horse mackerel [starting now]."

たくさん means a lot: a big amount or a big number of something. Put it right before the verb (たくさん釣る, "catch a lot"), or before a noun with の (たくさんの魚, "lots of fish").

On this card, he's getting his bait ready and says how many fish he wants: たくさん.

たくさん itself never changes. Polite or casual only shows at the end: たくさん釣りたいです / たくさん釣りたい.

Examples

Monday morning, a coworker asks about your weekend.

週末はたくさん寝ました。

"I slept a lot over the weekend."

たくさん is about how much. For "very" with a describing word, it's とても: とても眠い, "very sleepy".

Polite

Telling a friend about the town festival.

お祭りにたくさんの人が来ました。

"Lots of people came to the festival."

Right before a noun, add の: たくさんの + 人.

Polite

Your roommate wants to go shopping. You check the fridge first.

卵、まだたくさんあるよ。

"We still have lots of eggs."

Very common spot: ～が たくさん ある / いる, "there are lots of ___".

Casual

Back from a trip, a friend asks if you took pictures.

うん、たくさん撮ったよ！

"Yeah, I took loads!"

Casual

With and without

Without

水を飲みました。

"I drank water." Just what you did.

With たくさん

水をたくさん飲みました。

"I drank a lot of water." Now you say how much.
